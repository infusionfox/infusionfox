How the calculation works

Pick per-drug doses, a pump rate, and a bag size, then compute the drug amounts so the bag delivers those doses for the entire time it runs.

  1. Pump rate (mL/hr) = weight (kg) × rate per kg.
  2. Bag duration (hr) = bag mL ÷ pump mL/hr.
  3. For each drug: total mg in bag = dose (mg/kg/hr) × weight × bag duration.
  4. Volume of stock to draw = total mg ÷ stock concentration (mg/mL).
  5. Remove a saline volume equal to the sum of the drug volumes, then add the drugs.
Rationale

Why this calculator is dog-only

Cats are more susceptible to lidocaine cardiotoxicity than dogs (depression of myocardial contractility and cardiac output, arrhythmias, and CNS effects can occur at doses below the MLK lidocaine target of 1.5 mg/kg/hr). The published MLK protocol does not include a feline lidocaine maintenance CRI, and InfusionFox locks the species selector to dog. For feline multimodal analgesia, consider fentanyl, buprenorphine, or hydromorphone as single-agent CRIs.

Monitoring during MLK CRI

Pain score, respiratory rate and effort, heart rate and rhythm (ECG), blood pressure, mentation, and body temperature. MLK CRIs have been associated with hypothermia in dogs (Wang et al. 2020), so check temperature during and after the infusion and provide active warming if needed. Lidocaine toxicity in dogs presents as muscle twitching, ataxia, seizures, and cardiac depression. If toxicity is suspected, discontinue and treat supportively.

Stepping down

Discontinue lidocaine and ketamine first when clinical signs allow. Morphine can be continued separately if longer analgesia is needed; the MLK morphine dose lands in the mid-range of Plumb's 0.1–1 mg/kg/hr maintenance range. For ketamine post-surgical maintenance, 2 µg/kg/min (≈ 0.12 mg/kg/hr) for 24 hr is a published transition dose.
