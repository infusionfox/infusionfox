How the calculation works

{# Standard-bag mode explanation. The JS toggle on the form also shows/hides these mode-section blocks down here, so the formula narrative always matches the form fields and result panel the user is currently looking at. #}
{% if drug.dose_unit.value == 'ug/kg/min' %}

{{ drug.display_name }} is dosed in µg/kg/min. To convert a per-minute dose into a per-hour CRI rate, multiply by 60. The full formula:

$$\text{mL/hr} = \frac{\text{weight}_{\text{kg}} \times \text{dose}_{\mu g/kg/min} \times 60}{\text{concentration}_{\mu g/mL}}$$

Why the 60? The patient's weight (kg) times the dose ({{ drug.dose_mass_unit }} per kg per minute) gives micrograms per minute. Multiplying by 60 converts to micrograms per hour. Dividing by the concentration ({{ drug.dose_mass_unit }} per mL of the prepared CRI) yields mL per hour.

{% elif drug.dose_unit.value == 'mg/kg/hr' %}

{{ drug.display_name }} is dosed in mg/kg/hr. Because the prepared CRI concentration is expressed in {{ drug.concentration_unit_label }}, the formula needs a × 1000 to convert the mg dose into {{ drug.dose_mass_unit }} before dividing:

$$\text{mL/hr} = \frac{\text{weight}_{\text{kg}} \times \text{dose}_{mg/kg/hr} \times 1000}{\text{concentration}_{\mu g/mL}}$$

Patient weight (kg) times dose (mg per kg per hour) gives mg per hour. Multiplying by 1,000 converts mg to {{ drug.dose_mass_unit }} so the units match the bag concentration. Dividing by {{ drug.concentration_unit_label }} yields mL per hour.

{% else %}

{{ drug.display_name }} is dosed in µg/kg/hr. The CRI rate calculation is:

$$\text{mL/hr} = \frac{\text{weight}_{\text{kg}} \times \text{dose}_{\mu g/kg/hr}}{\text{concentration}_{\mu g/mL}}$$

Patient weight (kg) times dose ({{ drug.dose_mass_unit }} per kg per hour) gives total {{ drug.dose_mass_unit }} per hour. Dividing by concentration ({{ drug.dose_mass_unit }} per mL) yields mL per hour. No unit-time conversion needed because dose and rate are both per-hour.

{% endif %}
{# Target-pump-rate mode explanation. Three-step preparation math: derive bag concentration from the fixed pump rate, compute total drug for the chosen bag volume, then derive volume of stock from the vial concentration. Only rendered when the drug supports target-pump-rate mode. #} {% if drug.supports_target_pump_rate_mode %}

In target-pump-rate mode the pump rate is fixed by the clinician and the bag concentration is the unknown. The preparation runs in three steps.

Step 1: bag concentration

Same numerator as standard-bag mode (the total drug delivered per hour for this patient), but divided by the chosen pump rate instead of by a chosen bag concentration:

{% if drug.dose_unit.value == 'ug/kg/min' %} $$\text{bag concentration}_{\mu g/mL} = \frac{\text{weight}_{\text{kg}} \times \text{dose}_{\mu g/kg/min} \times 60}{\text{pump rate}_{\text{mL/hr}}}$$ {% else %} $$\text{bag concentration}_{\mu g/mL} = \frac{\text{weight}_{\text{kg}} \times \text{dose}_{\mu g/kg/hr}}{\text{pump rate}_{\text{mL/hr}}}$$ {% endif %}

Step 2: total drug in the bag

The bag concentration times the chosen bag volume gives the total micrograms of drug to add. Convert to milligrams (divide by 1,000) for the size of stock you'll actually be drawing:

$$\text{total drug}_{\mu g} = \text{bag concentration}_{\mu g/mL} \times \text{bag volume}_{\text{mL}}$$
$$\text{total drug}_{mg} = \frac{\text{total drug}_{\mu g}}{1{,}000}$$

Step 3: volume of stock to draw

Total drug divided by the stock vial concentration gives the volume of stock to draw and add to the bag:

$$\text{stock volume}_{\text{mL}} = \frac{\text{total drug}_{mg}}{\text{stock}_{mg/mL}}$$

Worked through together: divide the total drug in the bag by the stock vial's mg/mL to get the mL of stock that contains that much drug. That is the volume to pull into the syringe and add to the bag.

{% endif %}
